Shopify and WooCommerce are two of the most popular ways to run an online store, and they represent two different philosophies. Shopify is a hosted platform: you rent a complete, managed system. WooCommerce is open-source software that turns a WordPress site into a store: you own and run it. Neither is "better" in general. Each is better for certain people.

## At a glance

| | Shopify | WooCommerce |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Hosted SaaS | Open-source WordPress plugin |
| Setup | Fast, guided | Needs hosting + WordPress |
| Maintenance | Handled for you | Your responsibility |
| Customisation | High, within the platform's rules | Practically unlimited |
| Cost structure | Monthly plan + payments + apps | Hosting + extensions + developer time |
| Content & blogging | Good | Excellent (it's WordPress) |
| Ownership | You rent the platform | You own the code and data |

## Ease of use

Shopify wins for beginners. You sign up, choose a theme, add products and connect payments in one dashboard. Security, hosting, updates and checkout uptime are handled for you, and there's 24/7 support.

WooCommerce needs a few more steps: buy hosting, install WordPress, install WooCommerce, choose a theme and configure extensions. Many hosts offer one-click installs, but you're still responsible for keeping WordPress, the theme and plugins updated and compatible.

## Cost: it's about structure, not sticker price

Shopify's costs are predictable: a monthly plan, payment processing fees, and often several paid apps. If you use an outside payment provider instead of Shopify's own, an additional transaction fee may apply. Check the current rates on Shopify's pricing page.

WooCommerce itself is free, but a real store has costs: quality hosting, a premium theme, paid extensions (subscriptions, bookings, advanced shipping), security tools and, often, developer time. Small stores on budget hosting can run cheaply; complex stores can cost as much as or more than Shopify.

## Flexibility and customisation

WooCommerce is open code. If you can imagine it, a developer can build it: unusual product configurators, custom checkout logic, membership-plus-store hybrids. You can also choose any hosting and any payment provider.

Shopify is highly customisable through themes, its app store and its developer tools, but some things are fixed by the platform, particularly around checkout on lower plans. For most stores this never matters; for unusual business models it can.

## SEO and content

Both can rank well. WooCommerce inherits WordPress's excellent content tools, which makes it a natural choice for content-led brands that publish a lot of articles. Shopify handles technical SEO basics well and has a capable blog, though URL structures are more fixed. In practice, product quality, content and links matter far more than the platform.

## Performance and scaling

Shopify scales with almost no effort: traffic spikes on big sale days are its problem, not yours. WooCommerce scales too, but performance depends on your hosting, caching and plugin choices. A poorly maintained WooCommerce site gets slow; a well-built one is fast.

## Security

On Shopify, platform security and PCI compliance are managed for you. On WooCommerce, you share that responsibility: keep everything updated, use reputable plugins, add a security tool and back up regularly. Most WooCommerce security incidents come from outdated plugins.

## The verdict

**Choose Shopify if** you want to focus on products and marketing, you don't have a developer, you plan to grow a physical-product brand, or you want predictable, managed infrastructure.

**Choose WooCommerce if** you already run a WordPress site, content is central to your strategy, you need unusual functionality, you want to own your entire stack, or you have reliable developer support.
