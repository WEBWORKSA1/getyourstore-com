Digital products are one of the most attractive ways to start selling online: no inventory, no shipping and very high margins once the product is made. The challenge shifts from logistics to creating something genuinely valuable and getting it in front of the right people.

## What digital products sell?

- **Templates:** spreadsheets, budgets, planners, resumes, social media and website templates
- **Design assets:** fonts, icons, illustrations, mockups, photo presets and filters
- **Education:** eBooks, guides, online courses, workshops and cohort programmes
- **Printables:** wall art, worksheets, planners, colouring pages
- **Audio and video:** music, sound effects, stock footage, meditation tracks
- **Software and code:** plugins, themes, scripts and apps
- **Memberships:** ongoing access to content, community or resources

The best ideas usually come from something you already know well and that people regularly ask you about.

## Validate before you build

Before spending weeks creating a course or a template pack:

- Check that people search for and buy similar products.
- Ask your audience what they're struggling with.
- Pre-sell: offer early access at a discount and build once people pay.
- Start small: a focused $15 template can validate an idea faster than a $300 course.

## Choose where to sell

- **Creator platforms** (Payhip, Sellfy, Gumroad and similar) are purpose-built for digital goods: file hosting, secure delivery, licence keys, memberships and simple storefronts. Fastest to launch.
- **Full ecommerce platforms** handle digital products via built-in features or apps. A good choice if you also sell physical products.
- **Marketplaces** for digital goods offer discovery, with lower prices and less control.
- **Your own site** plus a checkout tool gives the most brand control.

## Pricing digital products

Because the marginal cost is near zero, price on **value and positioning**, not cost:

- What outcome does the buyer get, and what is that worth to them?
- What do comparable products charge?
- Can you offer tiers (basic, standard, premium with extras or support)?
- Bundles of related products can raise the average order value significantly.

Avoid racing to the bottom. Cheap digital products attract bargain hunters and refund requests.

## Delivery and protection

- Use a platform that delivers files automatically after purchase, with expiring download links.
- Add licence terms (personal vs commercial use) to the product page and the files.
- Consider PDF stamping (adding the buyer's email to files) for premium products.
- Accept that some piracy happens. Focus on serving paying customers well rather than heavy restrictions that frustrate them.

## Tax on digital products

Digital products often have specific tax rules. For example, VAT on digital services to consumers in the EU and UK is generally charged based on the buyer's location. Some creator platforms handle this for you; check how yours works and consult a tax professional for your situation.

## Product pages for digital goods

Since buyers can't see a physical item, show:

- preview images or a sample (first chapter, a demo template, a video walkthrough)
- exactly what's included (file formats, page count, video hours)
- compatibility (which software or devices)
- who it's for, and who it's not for
- your refund policy, clearly stated

## Marketing digital products

- **Content marketing** works especially well: tutorials, tips and free samples that demonstrate your expertise.
- **Email list:** offer a free mini-product as a lead magnet, then a welcome sequence.
- **Social proof:** testimonials and examples of buyers' results.
- **Affiliates:** let others promote your product for a commission.
- **Launch windows:** limited-time launches create focus and urgency, honestly.

## Grow with a product ladder

Many successful creators build a ladder: a free resource → a low-priced starter product → a core product → a premium offer or membership. Each step builds trust for the next.
