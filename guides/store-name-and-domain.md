Your store's name shows up everywhere: the domain, packaging, social handles, ads and word-of-mouth. A good name makes everything a little easier. A bad one adds friction forever. Here's how to choose well without agonising for weeks.

## Five naming styles

1. **Descriptive** ("The Candle Shop"). Clear and instantly understood, but harder to trademark and to grow beyond.
2. **Evocative** ("Maple & Moss"). Suggests a feeling or aesthetic rather than a product. Flexible and brandable.
3. **Invented** (made-up or blended words). Unique and easy to trademark, but you must teach people what it means.
4. **Founder-based** (a person's name). Personal and authentic; works well for craft and premium brands.
5. **Compound or modified** (two words joined, or a word with a suffix). Often easier to find an available domain for.

## The seven tests a name must pass

- **The radio test.** If you say it aloud, can someone spell it? Avoid odd spellings, hyphens and numbers that could be written two ways.
- **Length.** Shorter is easier to remember and type. Under about 12–14 letters is a good target.
- **Room to grow.** Don't box yourself in. "Yoga Mats Direct" is awkward the day you sell blocks and apparel.
- **Meaning.** Check that it doesn't mean something unfortunate in another language or as slang.
- **Domain availability.** Ideally the .com, or a clear alternative you're comfortable with.
- **Social handles.** Available, or close variants, on the platforms your customers use.
- **Trademark clearance.** Not already used by someone for similar goods in your market.

## Choosing a domain

**.com is still the default.** Many people type .com automatically, and it's the most trusted extension worldwide. If you can get the .com of your name, take it.

**Country extensions** (.ca, .co.uk, .in, .com.au) can be great if you sell mainly in one country, and they signal local presence.

**Newer extensions** (.shop, .store, .co) can work, but expect some visitors to type .com by mistake. If a competitor owns the .com, that traffic goes to them.

If your exact .com is taken, try:

- adding a short word: *get*, *shop*, *try*, *hq*, *co*
- a slightly different name that passes the tests above
- making an offer for the domain, or buying a premium, already-registered domain. A short, memorable .com can make a new brand look established on day one.

Avoid hyphens, number–word confusion ("4" vs "for") and names that are a typo away from a big brand.

## Check trademarks before you fall in love

Search your national trademark office's database (and the [WIPO Global Brand Database](https://www.wipo.int/branddb/en/) for international markets) for identical or confusingly similar names in your product category. A domain being available does **not** mean the name is free to use. If your brand matters long-term, a short consultation with a trademark professional is cheap insurance.

## Lock it down the same day

Once you've decided:

1. Register the domain (and consider common variants).
2. Claim the social handles.
3. Set up a branded email address on your domain.
4. Turn on auto-renew and registrar lock, so you never lose the domain by accident.

## Common mistakes

- Spending weeks on the name instead of validating the product. A good-enough name and a great product beats the reverse.
- Choosing something too trendy that will date quickly.
- Using a competitor's brand name inside yours (a trademark risk).
- Letting the domain expire because the renewal went to an old email address.
