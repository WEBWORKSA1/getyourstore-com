Online shoppers can't touch your product, so photos do the job of the shop floor. The good news: modern phones are more than capable. Lighting, stability and consistency matter far more than the camera. Here's a setup you can build for the cost of a few supplies.

## What you need

- **A phone** with a decent main camera (use the main rear lens, not the ultra-wide or selfie camera)
- **A small tripod** or phone clamp. Stability is the biggest quality upgrade per dollar.
- **A background:** a roll of white or neutral paper, or a sheet of foam board curved into a "sweep" so there's no visible horizon
- **A bounce card:** white foam board to reflect light into shadows
- **A diffuser** for harsh light: baking paper, a white shower curtain or a thin white sheet

## Light: use a window

Natural light from a large window is the easiest professional-looking light you'll ever get.

1. Set up next to a window, out of direct sunlight. Bright overcast days are ideal.
2. If the sun is shining directly in, hang your diffuser over the window to soften it.
3. Place the product side-on to the window and put the white bounce card on the opposite side to lift the shadows.
4. **Turn off the flash and all room lights.** Mixed light sources create colour casts.

## Camera settings that matter

- Clean the lens (smudges are a common cause of hazy photos).
- Tap to focus on the product, then slightly lower the exposure if highlights look blown out.
- Avoid digital zoom. Move the phone closer, or crop later.
- Shoot at the highest resolution available.
- Keep the phone in one fixed position and **rotate the product** for different angles, so every shot is framed the same.

## The shots every product needs

1. **Hero shot:** the product on a clean background, filling most of the frame. It's your thumbnail, so it must read clearly at small sizes.
2. **Angles:** front, back, side and top where relevant.
3. **Detail shots:** texture, stitching, materials, ports, labels.
4. **Scale shot:** in a hand, being worn or next to a familiar object.
5. **Lifestyle shot:** the product in use, in the setting your customer imagines.
6. **What's included:** everything in the box, laid out.

Most stores benefit from at least three to five images per product. Short videos (10–15 seconds of the product in use) can help too.

## Consistency is what makes a store look professional

Use the same background, the same light direction, the same framing and the same aspect ratio (square is a safe default) for every product in a collection. A grid of consistent photos instantly reads as a real, trustworthy brand.

## Editing: keep it honest

Free apps can straighten, crop, brighten and remove small blemishes from the background. A background-removal tool is helpful for marketplace listings that require pure white backgrounds. Never edit in ways that misrepresent colour, size or quality. It leads to returns and bad reviews.

## Common mistakes

- Using flash, which flattens the product and distorts colours
- Busy backgrounds that compete with the product
- Shooting at an angle that distorts proportions (keep the phone level with the product)
- Low-resolution images that look soft when zoomed
- Inconsistent shots across products
