Your homepage gets attention, but your product pages make the money. Most shoppers land directly on a product from search, social or ads, and decide within seconds whether to stay. Here's what a page needs to turn that visit into a sale.

## Above the fold: answer three questions instantly

On a phone, before scrolling, a visitor should know:

1. **What is it?** A clear product title and a sharp hero image.
2. **What does it cost?** The price, plus any discount, clearly displayed.
3. **How do I get it?** A prominent "Add to cart" button and the key variant choices (size, colour).

If any of those is missing or hard to find, conversions suffer.

## The product title

Write titles for the search box, not the warehouse. Include what it is and the key attribute buyers search for: "Waterproof Recycled Daypack, 20L" beats "Harbor 2.0". Keep the brand flair for the description.

## Images and video

Use at least three to five consistent images: hero, angles, details, scale and lifestyle. Let shoppers zoom. Add a short video if the product benefits from motion or demonstration. See [product photography on a budget](/guides/product-photography-on-a-budget.html).

## Copy that sells benefits

Pair every feature with the benefit it delivers:

- *Feature:* "Recycled waterproof fabric". *Benefit:* "Rain and spills are no problem, and it's better for the planet."
- *Feature:* "640 g". *Benefit:* "You'll barely notice you're carrying it."

Structure the description for skimming: an opening line about the outcome, a short paragraph, then bullet points for features and specs. Use the words your customers use (read reviews of similar products for their language). The [description writer](/tools/product-description-writer.html) gives you a first draft.

## Price presentation

- Show the price near the title and the button.
- If you discount, show the original price and the saving clearly. Never invent a fake "was" price; it's misleading and illegal in many places.
- Mention payment options (wallets, buy-now-pay-later if offered) near the button.
- Show bundle or volume pricing where it raises average order value.

## Remove risk: shipping, returns and trust

Uncertainty kills purchases. Near the add-to-cart button, state:

- the delivery estimate and shipping cost (or the free-shipping threshold)
- the returns window in plain language, linked to your full policy
- secure checkout and accepted payment methods

## Social proof

Real reviews are among the most persuasive elements on a page. Ask every early customer for one, and show star ratings near the title. Photo reviews are especially powerful. Never write or buy fake reviews: it's deceptive, often illegal, and platforms penalise it.

## Answer objections with an FAQ

Add a short FAQ to each product page covering sizing, materials, care, compatibility, what's included and delivery. It reassures buyers, reduces support emails and gives search engines more relevant text.

## Variants and stock

Make variant selection obvious, using swatches for colours and clear size buttons with a size guide. Show low-stock messages only when they're true.

## Mobile first

Check every product page on a phone:

- Is the add-to-cart button visible without hunting?
- Do images load quickly?
- Can you choose variants easily with a thumb?
- Is text large enough to read without zooming?

A sticky add-to-cart bar on mobile can help on long pages.

## Cross-sells and next steps

Below the main content, suggest complementary items ("Complete the set") and recently viewed products. Keep it relevant; random suggestions add noise.

## Measure and improve

Track each product's conversion rate (orders ÷ product page visitors). If a page gets traffic but few sales, test one change at a time: a better hero image, a clearer benefit headline, shipping info moved higher or a lower free-shipping threshold.
